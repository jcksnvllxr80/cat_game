  ---
  Paws of Destiny — Strategic Game Design Review

  Vision Statement

  A charming 2.5D adventure game where a team of cats embarks on an epic quest to confront the fearsome Dog King — only to discover that kindness and understanding matter more than
  fighting. Designed for elementary-aged kids (ages 6-11), the game teaches teamwork, resource management, and the lesson that "scary" things aren't always what they seem.

  ---
  Story Arc (Expanded)

  Act 1 — "The Call" (Levels 1-3)

  - You play as Whiskers, a brave orange tabby from a small cat village called Purrville
  - The Dog King's shadow army (squirrels, raccoons, and crows doing his bidding) has been stealing fish from the village
  - The village elder, Grandma Mittens, reveals that an ancient Puzzle Map — split into 7 pieces — shows the path to the Dog King's fortress
  - First 3 levels teach movement, jumping, and Whiskers' special ability before earning your first companion

  Act 2 — "The Journey" (Levels 4-9)

  - Each map piece is guarded by a mini-boss or puzzle challenge
  - You meet a new cat friend roughly every 2 levels
  - NPCs along the way say increasingly ridiculous things about the Dog King:
    - "He's as tall as THREE houses stacked on top of each other!"
    - "He once barked so loud it knocked over a mountain!"
    - "His teeth are made of DIAMONDS!"
  - The kids will start to suspect the twist — and that's the fun

  Act 3 — "The Reveal" (Final Level)

  - You reach the fortress — it's enormous and intimidating
  - Inside... sits a tiny, shivering Chihuahua named King Biscuit on an oversized throne
  - He didn't send anyone to steal fish — the shadow army was acting on their own because they were hungry too
  - Resolution: the cats and King Biscuit work together to create a shared food market so nobody goes hungry
  - Moral: Don't judge others by their reputation. Talk to people before assuming the worst.

  How Friends Join

  Each friend is found in trouble — you solve a small rescue puzzle to save them:
  - Luna is trapped in a dark cave (use Whiskers' pounce to break the rocks blocking the exit)
  - Boots is stuck on an island surrounded by water (puzzle: build a bridge from floating logs)
  - Cleo is tangled in vines high up a cliff (puzzle: find the right sequence to cut ropes)
  - Mochi is stuck in a hole (puzzle: find enough food to lure him out — he won't budge when hungry)

  Core Mechanics (Detailed)

  Hunger & Thirst System (Kid-Friendly)

  Keep it simple and visible — two big meters on screen (a fish icon for hunger, a water drop for thirst):

  ┌─────────────────┬────────────────┬──────────────┐
  │    Activity     │  Hunger Drain  │ Thirst Drain │
  ├─────────────────┼────────────────┼──────────────┤
  │ Sitting/Idle    │ 1x (very slow) │ 1x           │
  ├─────────────────┼────────────────┼──────────────┤
  │ Walking         │ 2x             │ 1.5x         │
  ├─────────────────┼────────────────┼──────────────┤
  │ Running         │ 4x             │ 3x           │
  ├─────────────────┼────────────────┼──────────────┤
  │ Using abilities │ 3x burst       │ 2x burst     │
  └─────────────────┴────────────────┴──────────────┘

  - When meters hit 25%: screen edges get slightly gray, cat meows sadly (warning)
  - When meters hit 0%: cat sits down and won't move — NOT a game over, just frozen until you feed them (no death/punishment — this is a kids' game)
  - Food/water is plentiful and visible — tuna cans sparkle, water bowls glow blue
  - No permadeath, no harsh punishment — the system teaches resource awareness without stress

  What Makes This Kid-Friendly

  1. No violence/death — enemies are "scared away" not defeated. Cats never die, they just sit down when tired/hungry
  2. Positive moral — the "villain" isn't evil, teaching kids not to judge
  3. Cooperative theme — friends help each other, every cat is needed
  4. Simple UI — two big meters, two accessory slots, clear visual cues
  5. Forgiving mechanics — food is plentiful, no game-over screens, generous checkpoints
  6. Humor — the increasingly absurd Dog King rumors will make kids laugh
  7. Exploration rewarded — hidden accessories and secret paths encourage curiosity

  Technical Scope

  For a 2.5D game targeting this audience, I'd recommend:
  - Engine: Godot (free, great 2.5D support, lightweight) or Unity
  - Art style: Hand-drawn/watercolor aesthetic — warm, friendly, storybook feel
  - Platform: PC first, with potential for Nintendo Switch (perfect audience match)
  - Estimated scope: 7 worlds x ~3 levels each = ~21 levels + boss encounters + story scenes
